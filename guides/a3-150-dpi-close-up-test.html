A3 at 150 DPI: What It Looks Like at 30 cm

By Jet Hu · Last updated: October 23, 2026

150 DPI is a common recommendation for large-format posters. This test asks a stricter question: if you inspect an A3 print at 30 cm, how much detail actually survives?

Test conditions

Print sizeA3 - 297 x 420 mm, printed at 100% with no scaling
Resolution150 DPI target
PaperRC luster (velvet) photo paper; GSM not listed
Ink / serviceCanon pigment ink, Taobao print shop, same batch as the A4 tests
Photo distance30 cm close-up only

What is readable at 30 cm

The large headings and 10 pt-and-up text remain readable. At 5-6 pt, letters lose some edge definition and curves show visible steps. The grayscale wedge and colour patches remain easy to distinguish, but the finest line pairs merge into grey bands.

The chart also makes the difference between "readable" and "sharp" obvious: a heading can still be read while its edges look slightly fuzzy. That is why a print can pass a basic legibility check and still feel softer than a 300 DPI sheet when the two are compared side by side.

Important: A3 posters are normally viewed from more than 30 cm. This test deliberately uses a close inspection distance and should not be read as "150 DPI is always bad for posters." We have not photographed this sheet at 1 m or 3 m.

What to do if you need an A3 poster

  • Use vector text and vector logos wherever possible - they stay sharp regardless of raster DPI.
  • Keep small text at 10 pt or larger if the file is only 150 DPI.
  • Use 200-300 DPI when the poster will be inspected closely or contains small type.
  • Ask the print service for their pixel requirement; pixels are less ambiguous than a DPI label.

Why A3 is a useful test size

A3 (297 x 420 mm) has exactly twice the area of A4 and is a common small-poster format. At 150 DPI it requires 1754 x 2480 pixels. The print in this test contained that many source pixels; the softness at 30 cm therefore comes from the limited pixel density rather than from a small file being stretched by the printer. That distinction matters when you are deciding whether to upscale a file or simply choose a lower DPI for a larger product.
