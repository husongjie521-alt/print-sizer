How to use this chart

  1. Find the size your print service offers, for example 16 × 20 in.
  2. Select the DPI the service recommends.
  3. Check that your image is at least as large as the pixel column, and ideally larger with the same aspect ratio.

Why DPI changes the pixel count

A printer places a fixed number of dots per inch. At 300 DPI a 6 × 4 in photo needs 1800 × 1200 pixels; at 150 DPI the same physical print only needs 900 × 600 pixels because each inch contains fewer dots. Physical size stays the same - only the required resolution changes.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution do I need for an 8 × 10 print?

At 300 DPI you need 2400 × 3000 pixels. At 200 DPI you need 1600 × 2000 pixels.

What is the largest size I can print with a 12-megapixel photo?

A 12-megapixel image is typically 4000 × 3000 pixels. At 300 DPI that prints at about 13.3 × 10 inches; at 200 DPI it prints at 20 × 15 inches.

Can I use a lower DPI for a bigger poster?

Yes, if the poster will be viewed from farther away. Large-format posters are often printed at 100–150 DPI, and billboards can be much lower because viewing distance is large.
